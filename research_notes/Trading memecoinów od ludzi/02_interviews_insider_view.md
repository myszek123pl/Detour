## 1. How the market works from the inside

### Cabals, KOLs, paid promotion (Bloomberg, Mar 2025, via CryptoSlate/Bloomberg Law summaries)
- Bloomberg: Solana attracts controversial memecoins because of an interlocking network of participants behind creation, launch and sale. "Cabals" specialise in launching tokens and connect KOLs (influencers) with token creators. Example named: Kelsier Ventures / Hayden Davis (Libra, Melania, Enron coins; he confirmed involvement). Contested legally; treat specific-token claims as allegations.
- KOLs promote because they hold the token or were paid (in project tokens, stablecoins, other crypto) (DePaul DePaul Crypto paper coverage).
- Bloomberg's sources: memecoin launches are marketed as the fair alternative to VC tokens but often carry as much or more insider advantage; Animoca's head of tokenomics said most claim "fair launch" but it is rarely true.
- Cointelegraph: if a community pushes back on one KOL, the cabal simply moves to another KOL (single-source, unverified).
- Insiders get cheap large allocations and sell into retail inflows (DePaul paper).

### Bundles and snipers
- DL News: insiders pre-load wallets with large amounts of a new token via stealth allocations / on-chain obfuscation ("bundles").
- Pine Analytics: deployers fund sniper wallets that buy in the same block as launch.
- Cointelegraph explainer: sniper bots monitor liquidity and act faster than humans.
- Implication practitioners draw: check holder distribution, funding-source clustering, same-block buys before entering.

### Reading "crime" on-chain (Cupsey-derived guide; secondhand)
- Hugging Face user guide summarising Cupsey (a top Solana trader): spotting "crime" is a core skill: dig into wallets, price action, identify which groups/"mini-cabals" push a coin; they often reuse the same wallets, so they are identifiable. Unverified, secondhand.
- PANews analysis (via TechFlow): Cupsey is mainly high-frequency front-running: buys at launch, sells within a minute or tens of seconds; ~95,000 trades in 30 days; largest loss (HAT token) came from sizing up and re-entering after a first loss; not an overnight holder. Same source says he sometimes builds positions in promising early tokens over time.
- Guide claims he may have shifted the Solana meta toward faster quick-flips and his fame drew copy traders (copy-trading him blindly is warned against). Low reliability.
- Anecdote: his group "Pioneer" had a full thesis on a token at ~60K mcap but only one member held it (illustrates conviction/execution gap).

### Narrative and attention
- Interview summaries: memecoins trade on narrative and simplicity, not fundamentals. One "In the Trenches" podcast guest: selection starts with "is it funny / does it have shock value". (secondhand summary)
- One Solana trader (In the Trenches podcast, ran accounts to 7 figures and lost them) avoids sniping brand-new sub-~$100K mcap launches; prefers a higher mcap range after some traction.
- CoinCub: the social feed of others' wins distorts how everyone feels about own portfolio (attention/FOMO driver).

## 2. What separates the few winners (as claimed by practitioners)
- Dedicated risk bankroll: Satsdats (fomo.family profile) loads a fixed amount (~100K mentioned) into a wallet and treats it as fully losable; enters in ~10K clips, no DCA; exits using 9-EMA on 1h (cuts when price loses it); does not care if price goes higher after selling.
- Position sizing as "Achilles heel": "Wood"/notanicecat69 (fomo.family AMA, "$4K to millions", WIF position reached 7 figures) keeps ~2.5% of net worth in the active memecoin wallet and sold his full position between $25M and $40M mcap. Handle-to-person link not independently verified.
- Speed + volume (Cupsey style) vs. conviction holders: two distinct styles; the first needs tooling/latency and discipline, the second needs narrative sense.
- Wallet/group intelligence: tracking KOL and smart-money wallets as confirmation (not as blind copy).
- Losers' traits: copy trading without understanding narrative, rage/revenge trading, sizing up after wins or losses, holding through KOL dump.
- Generic guides (low value, not interviews): ladder sells (e.g. 25% at 2x/3x/5x/10x), 1% sizing, daily loss limit 2-3R. Treat as folklore.

## 3. Learning curve and money lost
- SozAI summary of a viral "$300 to $200K in one trade" video: first month all losses from copy trading without narrative understanding, rage-trading, repeatedly zeroing out before discipline; quit day job ~2 weeks before the interview. Survivorship bias flagged by the summary itself. AI-generated; verify.
- Satsdats: ran accounts up to life-changing money and lost it 3-4 times; money starts feeling like "chips". (fomo.family)
- Coyote (fomo.family, "$250K loss" lessons): came into Ethereum memecoins as break-even/losing trader; distinguishes "notional tilt" (discomfort with dollar amount lost) from "ego tilt" (frustration at being wrong).
- Da Gen (Odaily, Chinese civil servant): large bets, big losses; from Aug 2025 took bank loans, debts ~1M RMB. Cautionary case.
- Benzinga (Mar 2025): trader turned ~0 into ~$5M and lost it all on memecoins ("I lost everything"). Headline-level only; I did not see details.
- Timeframes: sources imply months of losses before consistency and repeated full blow-ups over 1-3 years; no rigorous number found. Do not cite a precise "years to profitability".
- Base rate: Analytics Insight claims 90-95% of memecoin traders lose; unsourced, treat as rough folklore. (Look for Chainalysis/Dune/Pump.fun wallet-level data in another note.)

## 4. Psychology and burnout
- Tilt taxonomy (Coyote): notional vs ego tilt.
- Money-as-chips detachment (Satsdats) leads to oversizing after wins.
- Wider crypto-trading mental-health literature (not memecoin-specific): 2025 survey of 700 crypto traders reported 33.7% problematic gambling criteria, 33.9% at risk (via EuropeanGaming; verify original); Turkish cross-sectional study (n=437) worse sleep/stress in crypto traders (via KevinMD); Forkast clinicians: compulsive trading is "getting the trade on" not the money. Others: Vice "Crypto Ruined My Life", Yahoo "Insomnia, addiction, depression", Cointelegraph Magazine addiction piece.
