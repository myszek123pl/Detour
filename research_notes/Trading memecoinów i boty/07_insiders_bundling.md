# Insider Activity, Dev Wallets, Bundled Launches and Launch Sniping in Memecoins — and How to Detect Them On-Chain

> Research-process note for the report writer: In this session, page fetching was blocked for every domain tried (bitquery.io, soliduslabs.com, dev.to, docs.mobula.io, arxiv.org), and the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. All findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Treat exact figures as "reported by X" and check them against the primary pages before publishing. Not covered because of these limits: Solsniffer methodology, Bubblemaps' September 2026 feature details, and the arXiv paper "From Hype to Collapse" (2603.24625).

## 1. Insider tactics: bundling, dev multi-wallet supply control, cabal coordination, serial ruggers

### Takeaway
Insiders capture most of the profit at launch. On Pump.fun, the dev buys from several of their own wallets in the creation block, often atomically through a Jito bundle with the create transaction. This gives them a large share of supply at the launch price, which they spread across other wallets and sell into later buyers. Above the single-dev level, linked "clusters" fund many fresh wallets from one source (often a CEX withdrawal or cross-chain bridge) to snipe and control supply. The same clusters show up across many launches, so serial deployer/cluster history is a strong signal.

### Cited Findings
**Bundling mechanics**
- A Solana bundle is a group of up to five transactions that Jito-backed validators execute atomically in one block: either all succeed in order or the whole group fails. When liquidity funding and early large buys share one Jito bundle ID, the developer likely sniped the token. — [Datawallet: Solana bundles explained](https://www.datawallet.com/it/crypto/solana-bundles-explained-and-checking-risks)
- Pump.fun launch APIs/services openly offer "atomic token launches with Jito bundles", so create plus multi-wallet buys can go in a single bundle. — [PumpDev / Medium](https://medium.com/@pumpdevio/pump-fun-api-atomic-token-launches-with-jito-bundles-f60c2fc6be3e); [PumpDev create-token API](https://pumpdev.io/create-token)
- Bitquery defines "block 0" as the block that holds a coin's first trade. Its bundle numbers count the dev plus bundled wallets on launches with a bundle, and they are a floor because wallets funded by a separate transfer are not counted. Dev wallets that buy later or in a separate block are classed as snipers or unlinked buyers. Bitquery also notes that a launch service sending bundles for its customers would look the same on-chain, and that some launches don't use Jito, so block position is a strong signal but not proof. — [Bitquery investigation (via search summary)](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Some teams bundle-buy a supply allocation that they later hand out to community members, contest winners or early supporters. GMGN labels these wallets "bundler" (bot-bundled buy). — [madeonsol: Solana bundling explained](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-bundling-explained); [GMGN skills docs](https://skillsmp.com/skills/gmgnai-gmgn-skills-skills-gmgn-token-skill-md)

**Dev multi-wallet supply control and distribution**
- On launches where dev wallets bought in the launch block, the dev's wallets held a median 10% of supply, bought at the launch price. Bundled wallets passed tokens to other wallets, which sold into the pool. — [Bitquery (via search summary)](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- Pine Analytics ("Exit Liquidity Machines") documented deployers sending SOL directly to sniper wallets that then bought in the creation block. In one example, a deployer funded three wallets and all three bought the new token in the creation block. Pine separately documented multi-hop routing through 5–7 intermediary wallets to hide the funding link. — [Pine Analytics Substack](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); [bee.com summary](https://www.bee.com/51316.html); [bitcoinethereumnews summary](https://bitcoinethereumnews.com/tech/15000-solana-memecoins-manipulated-in-launches-using-pump-fun-report-finds/)
- A Bitquery case study (KIMCHI) describes three linked wallets that dumped the original token and sniped a copycat launch for about $104K. — [Bitquery: KIMCHI investigation](https://bitquery.io/investigations/kimchi-pumpfun-launch-snipers); [AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/pump-fun-revenue-kimchi-developer-profits)

**Cabal / cluster coordination**
- LIBRA (Feb 2025): Bubblemaps traced wallet 0xcEA, which had funded the $MELANIA deployer, to funding the main LIBRA creator wallet (DEfcyK). The link rested on cross-chain transfers, CEX deposit overlap and shared funding patterns. The cluster reportedly took about $6M by sniping through side wallets funded via cross-chain transfers from Arbitrum and Avalanche, and about $87M was drained in total. Bubblemaps said the same cluster showed similar launch behaviour in $TRUST, $KACY, $VIBES and $HOOD. Attribution to specific people is Bubblemaps'/analysts' claim. — [Bubblemaps blog: LIBRA playbook](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/content/43763660/bubblemaps-analysts-confident-libra-meme-coin-tied-to-melania-creators)
- MYSTERY (May 2026): Bubblemaps reported that 90 newly funded wallets bought 90% of supply at launch. All were funded by one wallet ("0x544E") that had earlier withdrawn 20 ETH from Binance and distributed it. The cluster sold about $100K and still held about 40% of supply. Bubblemaps called it a "textbook scam". — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster?amp=&amp=); [CryptoMorningPost](https://cryptomorningpost.com/2026/05/06/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-token-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping/)
- Other 2026 Bubblemaps cluster cases (allegations, not adjudicated): a cluster that sold about $600K of CWU while keeping about 85% of supply. Trader "Cupsey", alleged to have extracted about $1.74M via about 30 connected wallets. A Pump.fun influencer case (about $125K), where reporting stresses that chain data alone does not prove control of every wallet. "Cash Cat" (Nov 2026), with $2.7M of buys by a cluster of fresh wallets. — [CryptoBriefing (Cupsey)](https://cryptobriefing.com/bubblemaps-alleges-cupsey-solana-wallet-scheme/); [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/security/33573847/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster?amp=&amp=)

**Serial ruggers / deployer farms**
- An X analyst thread claims one wallet made 171k+ Pump.fun deploys with about 1% graduation, and argues most Pump.fun participants are "providing liquidity for a small group of serial deployers". This is a single-source social claim, not verified. — [GEM DETECTER on X](https://x.com/gem_detecter/status/2110610873970737600)
- Coinpaprika's education piece lists "deployer farms" alongside sniping and wash trading as launchpad red flags. — [Coinpaprika: Launchpad red flags](https://coinpaprika.com/education/launchpad-red-flags/)
- Pine Analytics recommends a persistent high-risk marker on wallets with a history of same-block sniping, especially those linked to deployers through direct or multi-hop funding. — [Pine Analytics on X](https://x.com/PineAnalytics/status/1914301091337953734)

### Inferences
- The usual extraction pipeline: deployer funds N wallets (directly, via multi-hop, or via CEX/bridge) → create and buy in one Jito bundle or the same block → tokens move to more fresh wallets to make holders look dispersed → staggered sells into retail demand.
- Since the same clusters repeat across launches (LIBRA→MELANIA→others; serial deployers), a wallet-reputation database of deployers, funders and early buyers that persists across tokens is probably worth more than any single per-token metric.

### Gaps
- No primary-source data on how often clusters launder funding through CEX withdrawals versus direct transfers versus bridges.
- No verified aggregate statistics on "serial rugger" prevalence (for example, the share of launches from deployers with 10+ prior launches). The 171k-deploy claim is unverified.

## 2. Detection heuristics a bot can apply before buying

### Takeaway
The most defensible pre-buy signals:
- Same-block / same-bundle buying with the create transaction.
- Funding-graph links between the deployer and early buyers, checked several hops deep including a shared CEX/bridge source.
- A high share of early buyers being freshly funded wallets.
- Supply still held by snipers, bundlers and insiders.
- The deployer's (and funders') history of prior launches.

Every tool's measurement is a lower bound or heuristic, and tools disagree a lot. A bot should compute its own metrics with explicit definitions.

### Cited Findings
- **Same-block / same-bundle:** Check whether creation and large early buys share a Jito bundle ID (verifiable in Jito Explorer). Solscan holder distribution and Birdeye bubble maps are suggested as cross-checks. — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/it/crypto/solana-bundles-explained-and-checking-risks)
- **Block-0 definition:** Bitquery uses "block 0 = the block containing the first trade". Dev-linked wallets in block 0 count as bundled. Transfer-funded wallets are missed, so the numbers are a floor. — [Bitquery (via search summary)](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)
- **Funding-source clustering, direct:** Pine kept only wallets that got direct transfers from the deployer before sniping in the launch block (a high-precision rule). Multi-hop routing of 5–7 hops is used to evade it, so deeper graph traversal is needed for recall. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); [bee.com](https://www.bee.com/51316.html)
- **Funding-source clustering, shared CEX/bridge:** In MYSTERY, all 90 sniper wallets traced to one funder that had withdrawn ETH from Binance. In LIBRA, links came from CEX deposit overlap and cross-chain funding (Arbitrum/Avalanche). — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster?amp=&amp=); [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-hayden-davis-rugged-libra-for-100m-with-president-milei/)
- **Fresh wallets:** "Newly funded" or "fresh" wallets buying large shares at launch were the core red flag in MYSTERY (90 fresh wallets, 90% of supply) and Cash Cat ($2.7M from fresh wallets). — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster?amp=&amp=)
- **Holder-graph links:** RugCheck's "Insider Networks" (beta) reportedly flags suspicious connections among top holders and bundled launches where a few linked wallets take most supply in the first blocks. Reviewers say it is a heuristic with false-positive risk on new, thin tokens. — [Solana Tracker: Rugchecks explained](https://www.solanatracker.io/blog/rugchecks); [smithii.io](https://smithii.io/en/top-3-solana-rug-pull-detection-tools/); [ChainAware](https://chainaware.ai/blog/best-web3-rug-pull-detection-tools-2026/)
- **Insider definition:** The InsightX API defines insider % as the share of supply held by wallets tied to the creator (for example dev-funded or known insider wallets), excluding the dev, and dev % as the deployer's share. — [InsightX docs](https://docs.insightx.network/reference/websocket-dex-metrics)
- **Sniper definition:** GMGN calls snipers wallets that "sniped the token at launch". Other guides define them as buying within the first few blocks. — [GMGN skills docs](https://skillsmp.com/skills/gmgnai-gmgn-skills-skills-gmgn-token-skill-md); [madeonsol: analyze holders](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-analyze-solana-token-holders)
- **Behavioural red flags:** A dev selling or moving tokens to fresh wallets. Large unsold sniper and bundler holdings that point to a coming coordinated dump. A rough benchmark of top-10 holders < 30% (excluding LP). These are guide heuristics, not standards. — [madeonsol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-analyze-solana-token-holders); [Polymart GMGN guide](https://polymart.app/crypto/gmgn-ai)
- **Unvalidated thresholds:** Vendor blogs give "5% normal / 15% coordinated" bundle thresholds. These are not peer-reviewed. — [solbundler bundle checker](https://solbundler.app/solana-bundle-checker); [scorplabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-bundle-detection)
- **Checkers disagree:** The same Pump.fun mint can score 8% bundled in one checker and 103% in another, because definitions differ (bundle ID vs same block vs same slot, current vs initial holdings, with or without dev). — [DEV Community article (via search summary)](https://dev.to/julio_627b13a09cd9ff90122/why-two-solana-bundle-checkers-give-you-two-different-numbers-2ph5); [Raiden bundle-checker guide](https://terminal.raiden.wtf/blog/pump-fun-bundle-checker)

### Inferences
These are proposed bot rules, built from the findings above. They are not validated thresholds.
1. **Creation-block analysis:** Compute (a) the % of supply bought in the create transaction's slot, (b) the % bought in the same Jito bundle as the create, and (c) the % bought in the first N slots (for example N=3). Keep these separate, because they measure different things.
2. **Funding graph:** For each early buyer (first ~50 buyers or first few slots), trace inbound SOL funding back 1–7 hops. Flag a cluster when several buyers share a common ancestor (the deployer, the deployer's funder, or one CEX hot-wallet withdrawal tx or batch) within a short window before launch.
3. **Fresh-wallet ratio:** Measure the share of early buyers whose first-ever transaction was a funding tx shortly before launch, and the supply they hold.
4. **Identical buy sizes / timing:** Look for many buys of exactly the same SOL amount, or with the same priority fee or compute-unit pattern, in the same slot. This is a cheap, plausible signal of scripted multi-wallet buying. No source in this research quantifies it (see Gaps).
5. **Holdings now vs at launch:** Insider/bundler/sniper % *still held* is the forward-looking dump risk. Tokens moved out of bundle wallets into new wallets should still count as insider-held.
6. **Deployer history:** Count the deployer's prior launches, graduation rate, and how much it sold after each. Do the same for the wallets that funded the deployer. Any link to known cluster wallets (a persistent blocklist, as Pine recommends) should be a hard veto.
7. **False positives:** Launch services that bundle for customers, and teams that bundle for later airdrops, look the same on-chain. So decide on (holdings + funding link + subsequent selling) together, not on bundling alone.

### Gaps
- No primary, quantified evidence was found on "identical buy sizes" or priority-fee fingerprints as a detection feature.
- I found no published precision/recall for any of these heuristics, and no validated cut-off thresholds.
- Withdrawals from the same CEX are a weak signal on their own, because many unrelated users withdraw from Binance and others. No source quantified the time-window or amount-similarity criteria that analysts use.

## 3. Quantitative evidence on how common insider-heavy launches are, and their outcomes

### Takeaway
Insider-heavy launches are common and profitable for insiders. Bitquery reports that dev-linked wallets bought in the launch block on about 21% of Pump.fun launches, and that devs plus bundles earned $579M+ net, against $31.5M for independent sniper bots. The wider launchpad base is overwhelmingly extractive: Solidus reports 98.6% of Pump.fun tokens show rug or pump-and-dump traits. That figure largely measures liquidity collapse, not proven fraud.

### Cited Findings
- **Bitquery on Pump.fun:** Dev-linked wallets bought in the launch block on 21% of launches (reported sample about 8.2M launches). On those launches the dev's wallets held a median 10% of supply. Devs and bundled wallets together came out ahead on 71% of bundled launches. On 98% of these launches, sellers took out at least 90% of what went in within 3 days. Devs and bundled wallets made at least $579M net of Pump.fun fees; sniper bots made $31.5M. The figures are floors. — [Bitquery (via search summaries)](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles); [KuCoin insight](https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/BTC/6adfa3a8172c2400070a9b10)
  - Conflict to note: Bitquery's page reportedly also says a non-dev wallet bought in block 0 of 38% of new coins over 11 months. This is a different metric from the 21% dev-linked figure. Reported periods also conflict ("launches to 30 June" vs "Oct 2025–Oct 7 2026"). Check the primary page.
  - Interpretation from KuCoin's coverage: the common "blame the snipers" narrative is backwards, because dev bundles earned roughly 18x what snipers did. — [KuCoin insight](https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/SOL/6ade9351b1e8a700077347ef)
- **Pine Analytics (about April 2025):** About 4,600 sniper wallets funded by about 10,400 deployers, roughly 15,000+ launches in a one-month sample, about 1.75% of Pump.fun launch activity (this share is reported inconsistently). Over 15,000 SOL realized net profit in the month. This counted only direct deployer→sniper funding, so it is a lower bound. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-meme-coin-snipers-systematic-problem/); [bitcoinethereumnews](https://bitcoinethereumnews.com/tech/15000-solana-memecoins-manipulated-in-launches-using-pump-fun-report-finds/)
- **Solidus Labs (May 2025):** About 98.6% of 7M+ Pump.fun tokens (Jan 2024–Mar 2025) were classed as rug pulls or pump-and-dumps, and only about 97,000 kept at least $1,000 liquidity. About 93% of about 361,000 Raydium pools showed soft-rug traits (median loss about $2,832, maximum $1.9M). Pump.fun disputed the framing. DeepStrike argues the metric is liquidity collapse below $1K, not proven fraud. — [Solidus Labs report](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/alarming-99-of-memecoin-launches-on-pumpfun-are-pump-and-dumps-or-rug-pulls-report); [CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says); [DeepStrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/rug-pull-statistics)
- **Academic base rates:** Pump.fun accounted for up to 71.1% of Solana tokens minted in Q4 2024, and fewer than 2% of tokens moved to major DEXs. — [arXiv 2512.11850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11850). A meme-coin manipulation study reports $3.27M realized losses from pump-and-dumps and notes multi-wallet use to hide ownership. — [arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01963)
- **Withdrawn academic evidence:** "Coordinated Sniper Cohorts on Pump.fun" (arXiv 2607.02795) reported 1,012 persistent wallet cohorts (about 1.57M buyer observations across 166,098 launches) and a 16.1% buyer-count lift. The paper notes that some results, including the 1,012 cohorts, are withdrawn pending correction. A later paper (arXiv 2609.18975) cites a "corrected release". Do not rely on these numbers. — [arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02795); [cryptobenelux](https://cryptobenelux.com/altcoin-nieuws/hoe-populair-is-een-memecoin-echt-nieuw-pumpfun-onderzoek-zet-vroege-walletactiviteit-in-ander-licht); [arXiv 2609.18975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18975)
- **Case-level outcomes:** LIBRA, about $87M drained in about an hour by one cluster. MYSTERY, 90% of supply sniped by 90 fresh wallets, about $100K sold, 40% still held. CWU, about $600K sold, about 85% of supply controlled. — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bubblemaps-flags-mystery-over-90-wallet-launch-sniping-cluster?amp=&amp=)

### Inferences
- For a buyer bot, the main base-rate point is this. About 1 in 5 Pump.fun launches has *detectable* dev-linked block-0 buying, and that is a floor. Insiders profit on most of those launches (71%). So a token with a detected dev bundle has a strongly negative expected outcome for later buyers unless there is other evidence.
- Snipers are a much smaller extraction channel than dev bundles ($31.5M vs $579M+). Detection should prioritise dev/cluster linkage over generic sniper counts.

### Gaps
- I couldn't get the Bitquery and Solidus primary pages (fetching was blocked), so the exact methodology, period and sample size are unconfirmed.
- No data on outcomes conditional on insider % *buckets* (for example, price performance of tokens with <5% vs >30% bundled), which would be ideal for setting thresholds.
- No Dune dashboard data was collected, because search ran out.

## 4. Tools that flag insider activity (Bubblemaps, GMGN, Trench.bot, RugCheck, Solsniffer)

### Takeaway
Retail tools expose the needed metrics: bundle %, insider %, dev %, sniper %, holder-cluster graphs and risk scores. But definitions are proprietary, undocumented or inconsistent, and the results can differ by an order of magnitude. Use them as inputs and cross-checks, not as ground truth.

### Cited Findings
- **Bubblemaps:** Bubble-map visualisation of linked holders. It publishes cluster investigations (blog, Intel Desk). In September 2026 it revamped the platform with a memecoin feed that screens for insider clusters and bundles and gives each token a score. — [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/09/16/bubblemaps-revamps-platform-with-memecoin-research-and-insider-filters/); [Intel Desk](https://intel.bubblemaps.io/); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/blockchain/bubblemaps-sets-a-new-industry-standard-for-catching-rug-pulls-before-they-happen)
- **GMGN:** The token page shows insider ("rat") %, bundle %, dev %, top-10 % and sniper counts, with wallet tags such as "bundler". GMGN also offers agent skills/CLI for programmatic token data. No official metric definitions were found. — [Polymart GMGN guide](https://polymart.app/crypto/gmgn-ai); [GMGN skills](https://skillsmp.com/skills/gmgnai-gmgn-skills-skills-gmgn-token-skill-md); [Datawallet GMGN explained](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)
- **Trench.bot:** A Telegram scanner (trench.bot). For a given mint it reports how much of the supply was bundled, how many wallets the bundle used, and how much they still hold, with a bubble-map viewer. No official methodology was found. A Rust crate (riglr-web-tools) wraps its API. — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/it/crypto/solana-bundles-explained-and-checking-risks); [BingX](https://bingx.com/vi/learn/article/how-to-use-a-solana-bundle-checker-for-safe-token-buys); [docs.rs riglr trenchbot.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/riglr-web-tools/latest/source/src/trenchbot.rs)
- **RugCheck:** A 0–100 normalised risk score with findings graded warn, danger or critical across 20+ on-chain checks. "Insider Networks" (beta) detects linked top holders and coordinated insider rings. Reviewers note false positives on new tokens. — [Solana Tracker](https://www.solanatracker.io/blog/rugchecks); [smithii.io](https://smithii.io/en/top-3-solana-rug-pull-detection-tools/); [Solana Compass: RugCheck](https://solanacompass.com/projects/rugcheck)
- **Data APIs for bots:** InsightX (insider %, dev %), Mobula ("detecting snipers & bundlers" almanac) and Bitquery (Pump.fun trades and new-launch streams) provide programmatic feeds. — [InsightX](https://docs.insightx.network/reference/dex-metrics-api-overview); [Mobula docs](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers); [Bitquery Pump.fun API](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Solana/Pumpfun/Pump-Fun-API/)
- **Verification:** Jito Explorer to confirm shared bundle IDs, plus Solscan and Birdeye bubble maps. — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/it/crypto/solana-bundles-explained-and-checking-risks)
- **Disagreement:** The same mint can read 8% bundled in one checker and 103% in another. — [DEV Community (via search summary)](https://dev.to/julio_627b13a09cd9ff90122/why-two-solana-bundle-checkers-give-you-two-different-numbers-2ph5)

### Inferences
- For an automated bot, third-party UI scores (GMGN, RugCheck) add latency and are opaque. Computing bundle and funding-graph metrics in-house from raw RPC or Geyser data (create slot, Jito bundle membership, funding hops) is probably more reliable and faster. Third-party APIs are better used as a secondary vote or for wallet-reputation lookups.
- Bubblemaps' cluster findings and Pine-style blocklists are most useful as seeds for a persistent "known insider cluster" wallet set.

### Gaps
- **Solsniffer:** I found nothing on its scoring methodology or its sniper/insider metrics, because search ran out first.
- No official GMGN, Trench.bot or RugCheck docs defining their metrics were found. All descriptions are third-party.
- I found no independent benchmark comparing these tools' accuracy against labelled rug or insider datasets.
